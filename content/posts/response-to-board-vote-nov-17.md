This morning, Chairman Jeff Mckay introduced a board matter proposing to further regulate stationary automatic license plate reader systems (ALPRs) in Fairfax County, VA. Today’s board discussion came as a surprise and disappointment. 

Deflock Fairfax represents a broad bipartisan majority of Fairfax residents demanding an end to the use of ALPRs. The Fairfax County Board of Supervisors have received nearly ten thousand emails from county residents and numerous requests to meet privately with community delegations and subject matter experts from local, regional and state organizations. Many of these requests have gone ignored, and we were neither informed of nor requested to provide additional input on today’s board matter

We stand at a critical time in our community’s history, a moment that demands genuine interest in community input, clear truth-telling, and a commitment to protect community members safety, privacy, and democratic freedoms. Moments like these require deliberate information gathering and genuine evidence-informed action. By continuing to use ALPRs and failing to include input from Deflock Fairfax community members, subject matter experts, and organizations that support the community’s demands, the county has further damaged the public’s relationship with law enforcement and the elected leaders we trust to represent our interests.

It has become more than clear that board discussions initiated by Chairman Mckay regarding ALPR use in the county are disingenuous, serve an agenda to maintain the status quo, and mislead Fairfax residents about the broad opposition to ALPRs. We are further troubled to learn there is coordination between  local and state leaders who represent Fairfax County to expand data sharing through regional partnerships, state leaders that support ALPR use and sit on the very Virginia State Crime Commission who made recommendations to broaden federal access to ALPR data earlier this month.

The community voice is essential to the preservation of our democracy. Like its decision to allow for the implementation of ALPR mass surveillance technology without community consent, today’s vote to continue the use of mass surveillance technology despite community demands to ban it signals that community dialogue is not central to the Board’s decisionmaking.
